
SYNTHETIC CREDIT POLICY
Knowledge Intelligence Platform — Retrieval Benchmark
Version: 1.0

IMPORTANT: This is fictional policy content created exclusively
for software testing and retrieval evaluation. It does not
represent a real financial institution or lending policy.

SECTION 1 — BORROWER IDENTITY VERIFICATION

Every applicant must provide valid government-issued photo identification before a credit application can proceed to the detailed assessment stage. Acceptable identification for this synthetic policy includes a national identity card or a valid passport. The credit officer must confirm that the applicant's full legal name, identification number, and date of birth are consistent across the application and the submitted identification document. Where the submitted information is incomplete, unclear, expired, or inconsistent, the application must be placed on hold until the applicant provides corrected documentation. Identity verification must be recorded in the application review history, including the document type examined, verification outcome, and date of review. No applicant may proceed to final credit approval while identity verification remains incomplete. Any suspected identity mismatch must be referred to the designated verification officer for further examination. The verification officer must document the reason for the referral and the final resolution before the application can continue.

SECTION 2 — REPAYMENT CAPACITY ASSESSMENT

Before recommending a loan, the credit officer must evaluate the applicant's monthly disposable income after existing debt obligations. The assessment must consider verified monthly earnings, recurring household expenses, outstanding loan repayments, and other material financial commitments. The proposed instalment must be assessed against the applicant's available disposable income to determine whether repayment is affordable. For this synthetic benchmark, the maximum permitted debt-service ratio is 40 percent of verified monthly gross income. Applications exceeding this threshold must not receive routine approval and must be referred for additional assessment. Income estimates supplied without supporting evidence must not be treated as verified income. The credit officer must document the income sources examined, existing obligations identified, repayment calculation, and final affordability assessment. A repayment schedule must be consistent with the applicant's documented income pattern. The institution must not recommend a loan solely because an applicant satisfies the minimum identification or credit-history requirements.

SECTION 3 — CREDIT HISTORY REVIEW

The credit officer must review available credit history information before making an approval recommendation. The review must identify unresolved defaults and delinquent repayment accounts, outstanding credit obligations, and patterns of repeated late repayment. The officer must distinguish between settled historical issues and current unresolved repayment problems. Where a credit record contains inaccurate, incomplete, or disputed information, the applicant must be given an opportunity to provide clarification or supporting evidence. The review outcome must record the relevant credit-history findings and explain how they affect the assessment of repayment risk. An unresolved default must be referred for further review rather than automatically treated as an approved exception. The credit officer must document the source and date of the credit information reviewed. A satisfactory credit-history assessment does not remove the requirement to verify identity and assess repayment capacity. All three assessment areas must be completed before a routine approval recommendation is made.

SECTION 4 — EXCEPTION ESCALATION AND APPROVAL AUTHORITY

Applications that do not satisfy a mandatory credit requirement must be handled according to the institution's exception procedure. Any unresolved policy exception must be escalated to the senior credit approval committee before a final approval decision is made. A loan officer must not independently waive a mandatory requirement or represent an unresolved exception as an approved application. The escalation record must include the policy requirement involved, supporting application evidence, the reason an exception is being requested, and the proposed risk mitigation measures. The committee must document its decision, including whether the exception is approved, rejected, or returned for additional information. An exception approval must identify the approving authority and any conditions attached to the decision. The application must retain an auditable record of the escalation and final decision. If the committee rejects the exception, the application must not proceed under the requested terms. Approval authority must remain within the limits assigned to each role.

SECTION 5 — APPLICATION RECORDS AND AUDITABILITY

Every credit application must maintain a complete record of the assessment and approval process. The record must include identity verification findings, repayment capacity calculations, credit-history review outcomes, and any exception decisions. Each review entry must identify the responsible officer and the date on which the assessment was completed. Supporting documents must be associated with the relevant application record so that an authorized reviewer can trace the basis of a decision. Changes to an application assessment must retain a record of the previous value, revised value, responsible user, and reason for the change. Final approval records must identify the approving authority and the applicable authorization level. Missing or contradictory assessment records must be resolved before an application is marked as fully reviewed. These controls support consistent decision-making, internal review, and subsequent audit examination.
